# ALL UNDER HEAVEN — CORE STORY DECISION v0.4
**Date:** 13 November 2026  
**Status:** Approved narrative direction; design only; no HTML change  
**Public communication:** Spoiler-free; game narrative/UI remains English

## Approved central arc
**A Nobody → A Soldier of Liu Bei → An Officer of Liu Bei → A General of Liu Bei → The Sixth Tiger General → The Successor of Liu Bei → The Unifier of China.**

This is the central trajectory of the novel RPG, grounded in *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* with historical chronology as a consistency framework. The protagonist is male, begins without an inherited political position, and earns status through actions and relationships. The game should dramatize his rise rather than merely assign titles after chapter transitions.

## Decisions adopted from conversation
1. Liu Bei is the protagonist's enduring leader and emotional mentor during the first major era. Do not present a regular rival-faction or independent regional crown as the main story.
2. The protagonist is **Liu Bei's intended successor**, taking over Shu-Han after Liu Bei's death around 223 CE **instead of Liu Shan** in the intended alternate-history main arc. This is a deliberate divergence, not a claim about real historical succession.
3. The former **Regional King** story and management mechanics are to be **repurposed** for administration and military/political leadership *within Shu-Han after Liu Bei's death*. They are not to be blindly deleted.
4. *The Sixth Tiger General* is a major earned, narratively recognized distinction, alongside the five famous generals in the novel tradition. Ensure it becomes possible only within a plausible shared-life window and with genuine accomplishments. Do not confuse the novel's Five Tiger Generals convention with a universally documented formal historical office.
5. The long-term ambition is Chinese reunification. Outcomes, methods, sacrifices, and player consent must be supported by branching, not free success flags. The player must be able to make meaningful military and political choices even within the Shu-aligned main trajectory.
6. The eight accepted protagonist portrait assets remain preserved and their milestones are redesigned for the new progression. The three legendary equipment items remain individually earned, potentially before General rank, never automatically granted by promotion.
7. Preserve existing applicable scenes, choices, NPC autonomy, romance, equipment, saves and endings while redesigning dependent politics. Do not start a game from scratch.

## Historical chronology constraints to resolve in writing
- **190–200:** service, early training, Xuzhou tragedy, and escalating instability of Liu Bei's forces.
- **200–208:** Liu Bei's transitions through Yuan Shao/Liu Biao; Zhuge Liang and Three Visits; role-appropriate protagonist knowledge and travel.
- **208–219:** Red Cliffs outcomes, Yi Province, Hanzhong, and emergence of prestige needed for the Sixth Tiger General milestone.
- **219–223:** court-level trust, relationship and achievement basis for extraordinary succession; prevent an unexplained last-minute replacement of Liu Shan. Care for established NPC deaths and the player's divergent world state.
- **223 onward:** protagonist's Shu-Han leadership, regional administration, campaigns, diplomacy, reform and the reunification ambition. Reuse old territorial/administrative work only after adapting its ownership and authority model.

## Outstanding design questions (NOT yet locked)
1. **Dynastic legitimacy:** adoption into Liu Bei's house, extraordinary designation of an outsider as heir, or a lawful successor-dynasty transition. Each has different titles and implications; do not silently treat them as interchangeable.
2. **Liu Shan:** a plausible political and personal status after exclusion from direct succession, with reactions from the Liu household, Zhuge Liang, officials, officers and people; do not remove him from the world without explanation.
3. **Succession eligibility:** exactly which already-earned acts of loyalty, leadership, character and political support provide credible reasons for Liu Bei's decision, and how difficult decisions can change that legitimacy.
4. **Agency/failure:** whether the succession is a required main-story milestone or an achievable branch with valid alternate conclusions if requirements are unmet. Player asked for the progression as the main concept; the success model is not yet specified.
5. **Unification strategy:** Wu-first, Wei-first, negotiated settlements and their consequences are options to be designed against the actual geopolitical state, not predetermined victories.
6. **Emperor outcomes:** protagonist's status as Shu-Han Emperor in 223 versus later sovereign of a reunified China must be distinguished. Maintain non-automatic victory and ending choices, with correct title logic.

## Impact assessment: v0.4.99.2m source (implementation deferred)
- Remove/rewrite independent kingdom *meaning*, not underlying viable story mechanics; adapt 225–250 content to Shu's governing institutions.
- Replace king-derived emperor prerequisites with earned, lawful Shu succession and state unification conditions; no unearned titles.
- Recontextualize kingdom name, seals, frontier authority, officer appointments, logs, conversations, UI, People entries and epilogues.
- Reassign (do not delete) two previously royal protagonist portrait phases, maintaining exactly eight approved portraits unless user explicitly changes that decision.
- Plan backwards-compatible handling of legacy saves; protect saved IDs and existing data. Test relevant branches and changed nodes incrementally.
- This document **supersedes the Regional King decision path in v0.1–v0.3** wherever inconsistent; it does not supersede the verified source files or establish unreviewed script details as canon.
