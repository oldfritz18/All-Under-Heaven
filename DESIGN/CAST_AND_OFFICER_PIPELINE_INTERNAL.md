# INTERNAL DESIGN — Chapter I Historical Cast & Future Officers

**Status:** editorial implementation tracker, not an automatic character unlock. Contains long-arc role concepts, not final promises of appointment.

## Chapter I narrative policy
- The player is a **16-year-old foot soldier**, enlisted under a legitimate chain of command, not Liu Bei's equal or a battlefield commander.
- Historical commanders make autonomous decisions before, during and after coalition combat. Only events personally witnessed can be held as protagonist memory. News about Sun Jian, Yuan Shao, Yuan Shu and Cao Cao is **reported**, not direct acquaintance.
- Liu Bei, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei remain commanders with their own priorities and working relationships. The protagonist experiences the battlefield at the left infantry wing under Han Sheng; no omniscient tactical narration.
- First chapter romantic thread: emotionally warm and age-appropriate; **no sexual conduct involving minors**. Adult intimacy in later years may be sensual, consensual and literary, without graphic descriptions.
- Humor should arise from recognizable human situations, without trivializing casualties.

## Recruitment / promotion mechanics for future batches
1. **Meet / know** in a location and year established by actual events.
2. **Earn trust** over repeated shared experiences, with independent NPC choices.
3. **Acquire authority** via a real appointment/promotion authorized by the current superior; no automatic rise based on chapter year.
4. **Offer role** with specific duties and scope; NPC may accept, negotiate, decline or leave.
5. **Prove command** through decisions, orders, morale, outcomes and casualty records; earn higher distinction through cumulative service.
6. **Synchronize** Story, Character, People, Military, Civic, Chronicle and save atomically.

## Story cadence
Chapter I builds service, common life, comradeship and the first understanding of leadership. Later chapters should support earned Sergeant/Captain milestones with their own ceremonies, witnesses, obligations, and human consequences. Heroic recognition is most moving when the protagonist recognizes those who stood beside him; it is never a free notification.
